# Intake and gates

Use this reference before an individual assessment. Ask in stages rather than requesting a full financial dossier at once. The student can invite a parent to confirm the financial section; do not demand bank statements, account numbers, government IDs, or other data that does not affect the recommendation.

## Opening statement

Explain that the exercise produces exploratory, evidence-based options, not admission, study-permit, scholarship, job, or financial advice. Ask the student to share only information needed to assess options and to avoid account details, identification documents, or other sensitive records. A parent or guardian should confirm numbers used for a feasibility conclusion when they fund the plan. Do not claim any particular data-retention, deletion, or confidentiality practice unless the platform makes it true.

## Information tiers

### Tier 1 — required before individual recommendations

- Intended entry year/term, current location and curriculum, current grade, and citizenship/residency context if it changes international-student requirements.
- Current/expected grades as ranges or relevant course results, relevant course completion, language-test status or plan, and academic constraints. Do not request transcripts or test certificates unless the user expressly asks to review them.
- Preferred subject areas and reasons; disliked subjects or deal-breakers; current career ideas expressed as interests, not predictions.
- Location constraints, accessibility/support needs the student chooses to disclose, family obligations, and preferred route types.
- Finance: available annual and total education budget; broadly available funds for study; dependable sponsorship; commitments that materially reduce availability; currency; tolerance for exchange-rate or funding uncertainty; whether loans are acceptable. Ask for ranges where precise amounts are unnecessary.
- Parent/guardian confirmation status for the budget and available funds.

### Tier 2 — ask only when it can alter a choice

- Interests, work/volunteer exposure, project examples, learning preferences, MBTI as optional reflection, campus/community preferences, and long-term geographic preference.
- Scholarship expectations, transfer preferences, language/foundation interest, practical-program interest, or need to work while studying.

### Do not request for this purpose

- Bank account numbers, login credentials, identification numbers, full financial statements, proof-of-funds documents, or unrelated family details.
- Health, disability, immigration, or family information unless the student volunteers it and it is necessary to assess an explicit constraint. Explain its use before relying on it.

## Finance and confirmation gate

The gate passes only when the student has provided enough figures to compare first-year and total-program cost and the budget status is parent-confirmed when a parent/guardian is involved in funding.

If the gate does not pass:

1. Do not name, rank, or recommend individual schools/programs.
2. Explain that affordability affects education planning and may matter to future study-permit preparation; do not assess permit eligibility or give immigration legal advice.
3. State exactly what is missing: for example annual budget, total budget, accessible funds, funding source, currency, or parent confirmation.
4. Offer general questions the family can discuss. Resume only after the missing decision-relevant facts are supplied.

If the gate passes but no verified candidate is affordable, do not lower the standard. Use the human-review handoff and explain the mismatch between confirmed budget and verified cost range.

## Intake quality checks

- Restate only the facts used for conclusions and ask the student to correct them. Do not repeat sensitive or unnecessarily detailed information in the final report.
- Flag conflicts, such as a route requiring a course the student has not taken or an annual budget that cannot cover the verified first-year range.
- Mark assumptions explicitly. Do not treat hoped-for scholarships, future income, favorable exchange rates, or admission results as confirmed funding.
